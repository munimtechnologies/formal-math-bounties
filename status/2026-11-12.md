## conjectures.io: the price of one attempt is stated two ways

conjectures.io (Bittensor Subnet 66) pays the first accepted, kernel-checked Lean proof of an open
statement. Checking a file is free, and submitting costs one credit. Two of the project's own
pages now give different prices for a credit:

| Source (read 2026-11-12) | What it says |
|---|---|
| conjectures.io/how-it-works | each attempt is "one credit, 0.25 τ"; pay "with TAO from a linked wallet or with other crypto through TMC Pay" |
| github.com/conjectures-io/conjectures-validator (README) | "a miner pays 0.5 TAO" per proof-verification attempt; "Every 0.5 TAO buys one credit" |

This guide (README section 2 and the 2026-11-10 status) has said 0.25 τ, which matches the
how-it-works page. It is not clear which page is current. **Before paying, read the price shown in
the submit step itself.** We have not paid for or made an attempt, so we cannot say which one the
site charges.

## conjectures.io figures, then and now

| Figure | 2026-10-14 | 2026-11-10 | 2026-11-12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problems in the catalogue | 274 | 306 | 306 |
| Open bounties (homepage) | - | 269 | 269 |
| Rewards paid (homepage counter) | - | 39 | 40 |
| Lean proofs accepted (homepage counter) | - | 53 | 54 |
| "Paid to solvers" (homepage, in US dollars) | - | $91,239 | $85,328 |
| Reward pool (how-it-works, in US dollars) | about $55,087 | - | $42,355 |

All dollar figures are the site's own conversions of the alpha token at the rate of the moment
("dollar figures are estimates at the current rate"). That is why "paid to solvers" fell between
11-10 and 11-12 even though one more reward was paid. The homepage counters are animated, so the
pairing of labels and numbers above was read from the page text.

**Paid since the last check (homepage "Recently solved", at the 11-12 rate):** Erdős problem 12,
part iii (11 Nov 2026, $4,530) and Erdős problem 156 (6 Nov 2026, $4,652), both to the same solver;
before that, two on 28 Oct 2026 ($771 and $1,773). The catalogue tags Erdős 12 part iii "Solved",
although its description text still ends "Open."

**How a bounty is sized (how-it-works and the validator README):** a new problem opens at 2.5% of
the available reward pool and rises to 12.5% over 15 days while it stays open; the amount is fixed
when you submit, and only the first accepted answer is paid. A proof of a faulty formal statement
can earn a capped "defect award" instead; the validator README caps it at the lesser of $750
(converted to alpha) and the locked bounty. Payment needs "an accepted proof and its chat logs": a
reviewer reads the original AI chat logs that produced the proof.

What this means for an individual formalizer: the per-problem bounty is a share of a pool whose
dollar value moves with a crypto token, and the pool is about 23% smaller in dollars than on
2026-10-14. Expect a few thousand dollars per open problem at today's rate, paid in alpha to a
Bittensor wallet, with an entry fee in TAO.
